AI, Cultural Safety, and Inclusive L&D

Guidelines for designing content that respects diversity, language, and Indigenous Data Sovereignty.

Designing for the Broad Spectrum of Diversity

AI models are notorious for amplifying existing societal biases related to gender identity, linguistic background, and religious beliefs. If we let AI draft a case study or generate an assessment rubric without human oversight, we risk embedding these biases directly into our organisational learning.

Explore the tabs below to see how to actively prompt and review AI outputs for different diverse groups across your workforce:

Migrants & ESL (English as a Second Language)

AI models often default to highly idiomatic, complex, or US/UK-centric English. This creates unnecessary cognitive load for learners who speak English as a second or additional language.

💡 AI is a great tool to assist in translation, but it does need to be checked for cultural nuance and accuracy.

L&D Action Points:

  • Prompt for Plain English: Explicitly instruct the AI: "Many of our learners are ESL, so use Plain English, avoid idioms, jargon, and colloquialisms."
  • Verify Cultural References: Remove AI-generated metaphors that rely on specific cultural knowledge (e.g., sports analogies like "hitting a home run" or "stepping up to the plate").
  • Combat Scenario Bias: Actively review AI outputs to ensure generated scenarios reflect diverse linguistic backgrounds without falling into biased stereotypes about migrant workers' roles or capabilities.

LGBTQI+ Inclusion

Default AI outputs often assume heteronormativity. Case studies will overwhelmingly feature heterosexual couples and binary genders unless specifically directed otherwise.

L&D Action Points:

  • Mandate Inclusive Pronouns: Instruct AI to use "they/them" or diverse pronouns in character briefs.
  • Normalise Diverse Families: Explicitly prompt for same-sex partners or chosen families in work-life scenarios.
  • Avoid the "Token" Trap: Integrate LGBTQI+ characters naturally into core business scenarios, not just standalone D&I modules.

Religious Diversity

AI models often default to generic secular or culturally Western norms. When creating learning scenarios, we want to ensure all religious backgrounds—whether Christian, Muslim, Jewish, Hindu, Buddhist, or any other faith—are respected and represented accurately.

L&D Action Points:

  • Inclusive Scenarios: If using AI to generate event planning, team lunch, or scheduling scenarios, explicitly prompt it to account for a variety of religious observances, holidays, and dietary requirements (e.g., Halal, Kosher, or fasting periods).
  • Broaden Perspectives: Review AI outputs to ensure they don't assume a single universal experience. Aim for a balanced mix of holidays and traditions (such as Christmas, Eid, Diwali, or Hanukkah) when creating general "family time" or "celebration" examples.
  • Respectful Representation: Ensure any mention of religious practices, clothing, or beliefs across all faiths is accurate, nuanced, and respectful. Fact-check AI to prevent caricatures or oversimplifications.

Intersectionality

People don't fit neatly into just one box. An employee might be an older, queer migrant. AI struggles with intersectionality, often addressing one facet of diversity while completely ignoring others.

L&D Action Points:

  • Layered Prompts: Don't just ask AI for a "diverse persona." Provide specific, multi-layered demographics to create realistic, intersectional case studies.
  • Check for Conflicting Bias: Sometimes AI will counteract a gender bias but introduce an age bias in the same paragraph. Human review must look at the whole picture.
  • Focus on Systemic Barriers: When generating leadership or conflict resolution scenarios, guide the AI to acknowledge how intersectional identities might experience workplace dynamics and communication differently.

⚠️ Poor AI-Generated Case Study

"Alex, a non-binary employee, is told by their manager that their passion for Pride month initiatives is a 'lifestyle focus' distracting them from 'actual work' and demonstrating a lack of 'traditional work ethic'."

Why this is poor: The AI uses coded, biased language ("lifestyle focus") and pits the employee's identity directly against their professionalism.

✅ Good AI Prompt Example

"Generate a leadership case study about scheduling a mandatory 3-day team offsite. The team includes:

• Sarah, who observes Shabbat and logs off by Friday afternoon.
• Ali, who requires prayer breaks and is fasting for Ramadan.
• John, who is organising his family's Easter weekend events.

Show how an inclusive manager proactively designs the offsite agenda, catering, and timing so all team members can fully participate without compromising their religious observances."

Why this works: Instead of asking for a generic "diverse team," this prompt provides specific parameters. It forces the AI to treat various religious practices equitably and frames them as standard planning considerations rather than workplace "issues."
